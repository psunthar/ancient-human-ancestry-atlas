Timeline Atlas of Ancient Human Ancestry

A hypothesis of human origins, migrations, and mixing of populations from David Reich’s book “Who We Are and How We Got Here”

Work in progress. This atlas shows the hypotheses in the book, as I read them, and a few later results. It is not a settled account of human history. Dates, lineages, routes and the size of the population circles are schematic and are still being reviewed.

Drag the slider from 300,000 years ago to the present. The map shows ice sheets, sea level, population ranges, migration routes and mixing events, with a few later results marked.

What is exact and what is approximate

  • Years are given as "kya", thousand years ago. Dates are rounded values from the literature. Ranges are shown where the estimates differ.
  • Sea level is the global stack of Spratt and Lisiecki (2016, Climate of the Past 12:1079), read at run time from data/sealevel_spratt_lisiecki_2016_0-300ka.csv. The stack gives +8.5 m for the present, so the atlas subtracts 8.5 m to make today 0 m. The curve is smooth because it combines many records with dating errors of several thousand years, and short events are blurred. After the shift, the last interglacial peak (about 121 kya) is about 5 m below today, whereas it is usually placed a few metres above today, and the fall toward the glacial comes earlier than in the hand-typed curve used in the first version of this page. Uncertainty grows with age (the 95% range is in the CSV).
  • Ice sheets from 0 to 80 kya are outlines of grounded ice (thicker than 10 m) from the PaleoMIST 1.0 reconstruction (Gowan et al. 2021, Nature Communications 12:1199; data: PANGAEA, CC BY 4.0). It has one time slice every 2,500 years and a 1-degree grid, so the outlines are coarse and are cross-faded, not morphed, between slices. Mountain glaciers are mostly too small to appear, and Antarctica is outside the map. Before 80 kya the ice comes from Batchelor et al. 2019, Nature Communications 10:3713, who mapped the best-estimate extent of the Northern Hemisphere ice sheets for whole marine isotope stages: 5a, 5b, 5c and 5d (72 to 117 kya), 6 (132 to 190 kya) and 8 (243 to 279 kya). Each outline is the extent at the height of its stage, and a stage can last tens of thousands of years, so the page shrinks the outline toward its centre in proportion to the sea-level drop within the stage. That shrinking is my simplification, not part of their data. Their maps are for mapping evidence at large scale and the authors warn against local-scale use. Where no stage outline exists (about 117 to 132, 190 to 243 and 279 to 300 kya, and everywhere in the Southern Hemisphere) the page draws the outline of the last glacial maximum shrunk in proportion to the sea-level drop. That is a schematic guess, not data. Outlines fade in and out over a few thousand years at the edges of each stage.
  • Land exposed by lower sea level comes from present-day sea-floor depth (ETOPO 2022, NOAA, public domain), sampled at 15 arc-minutes, smoothed, and grouped into depth bands of 0 to 30, 30 to 60, 60 to 90 and 90 to 130 m. A band fades in as sea level falls through its depth range. Uplift and sinking of the land under ice and water are ignored, so shorelines at 20 kya are approximate. Land and coast outlines on the map are still coarse hand-typed shapes, so exposed land can overlap or miss the coast by a few tens of kilometres.
  • Population bubbles show approximate ranges. Each bubble is drawn as an ellipse with a radius in ground distance (it looks wider on the map at high latitude, which corrects the stretching of the flat map), but its size is a rough guess, not a measured range. Positions and paths are schematic. The book describes ancestry and dates, not precise coordinates.
  • Routes through Southeast Asia: the solid line follows coasts and land that was dry at low sea level. Dashed lines are sea crossings. The Andaman Islands are drawn but are not on the route. Sources disagree on whether lower sea level fully joined them to Myanmar. Genetic estimates place the settlement of the Andamans after about 26 kya. Crossing details are from Bird et al. 2019, Scientific Reports.
  • The tags are my reading of which items are in the book. Check them against your edition. Items tagged "After the book" were published in 2019 (the Indus Valley genome from Rakhigarhi, and the South and Central Asia study). Africa after 60 kya, the Pacific islands, and Bantu expansions are not drawn.

For real data, see the interactive Sea Level Map (135,000 years of ice and sea level), Human AGEs and DORA (ancient DNA samples on a time slider). Sources for the South Asia items: Nature India on the Rakhigarhi genome, Shinde et al. 2019 (Cell) and Narasimhan et al. 2019 (Science, doi).
