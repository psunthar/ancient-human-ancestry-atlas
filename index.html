What is exact and what is approximate

  • Years are given as "kya", thousand years ago. Dates are rounded values from the literature. Ranges are shown where the estimates differ.
  • Sea level is the global stack of Spratt and Lisiecki (2016, Climate of the Past 12:1079), read at run time from data/sealevel_spratt_lisiecki_2016_0-300ka.csv. The stack gives +8.5 m for the present, so the atlas subtracts 8.5 m to make today 0 m. The curve is smooth because it combines many records with dating errors of several thousand years, and short events are blurred. After the shift, the last interglacial peak (about 121 kya) is about 5 m below today, whereas it is usually placed a few metres above today, and the fall toward the glacial comes earlier than in the hand-typed curve used in the first version of this page. Uncertainty grows with age (the 95% range is in the CSV).
  • Ice sheets use the outline of the last glacial maximum (about 21 kya) and shrink toward their centers in proportion to the sea-level drop. The real ice sheets grew and retreated with different shapes. Earlier glacial periods, such as 140 to 190 kya, had a different ice distribution, especially in Eurasia. Mountain glaciers and Antarctica are not drawn.
  • Land exposed by lower sea level uses rough outlines of the main continental shelves and shallow seas, each with a single depth threshold.
  • Population bubbles show approximate ranges. Their positions and paths are schematic. The book describes ancestry and dates, not precise coordinates.
  • Routes through Southeast Asia: the solid line follows coasts and land that was dry at low sea level. Dashed lines are sea crossings. The Andaman Islands are drawn but are not on the route. Sources disagree on whether lower sea level fully joined them to Myanmar. Genetic estimates place the settlement of the Andamans after about 26 kya. Crossing details are from Bird et al. 2019, Scientific Reports.
  • The tags are my reading of which items are in the book. Check them against your edition. Items tagged "After the book" were published in 2019 (the Indus Valley genome from Rakhigarhi, and the South and Central Asia study). Africa after 60 kya, the Pacific islands, and Bantu expansions are not drawn.

For real data, see the interactive Sea Level Map (135,000 years of ice and sea level), Human AGEs and DORA (ancient DNA samples on a time slider). Sources for the South Asia items: Nature India on the Rakhigarhi genome, Shinde et al. 2019 (Cell) and Narasimhan et al. 2019 (Science, doi).
